# Product

## Register

product

## Users

The platform serves Pipo Saude's internal variable remuneration workflow. Primary users are RevOps/Admin, Finance, Sales Managers, EVs, and CNs. RevOps/Admin users manage policies, goals, commission tables, imports, quarterly appraisals, contestations, and auditability. Finance users need confidence in payable totals, cash-flow projections, pending approvals, and exports. Managers need a read-only team view. EVs and CNs need clear visibility into their expected commissions, validation tasks, disputes, and payment timeline.

Users are working inside an operational finance context, often comparing numbers across periods, investigating exceptions, and making decisions that affect compensation. The interface should feel dependable during repeated use, not performative.

## Product Purpose

Pipo RV replaces manual spreadsheet-based commission operations with a centralized, role-aware platform for policies, financial imports, commission calculations, quarterly appraisal workflow, validation, approval, and exports.

Success means every stakeholder can understand what is owed, why it is owed, what still needs review, and where a number came from. The product should reduce manual reconciliation, make cash-flow and commission obligations visible, and preserve traceability across HubSpot data, financial spreadsheets, policy edits, workflow transitions, and final locked appraisals.

## Brand Personality

Reliable, precise, editorial.

The product should feel like a private financial statement translated into working software: calm, dense where the workflow needs density, visually disciplined, and serious about money without becoming cold or bureaucratic. It should preserve Pipo's internal domain language, including apuracao, apolices, comissao, agenciamento, EV, CN, and Finance, because those terms are how users reason about the work.

## Anti-references

The product should not look like a generic SaaS analytics template, a dark-mode finance cockpit, a colorful KPI toy, or a spreadsheet with nicer borders. Avoid decorative dashboards where each metric card gets a different color, charts that look like placeholders, gratuitous gradients, glass effects, novelty controls, and empty visual drama that does not help users verify financial truth.

It should also avoid hiding operational detail behind overly simplified visuals. Users need approachable complexity, not superficial minimalism.

## Design Principles

1. Make financial truth inspectable.
Every important number should expose enough surrounding context for a user to trust it: period, source, status, comparison, and drill path where relevant.

2. Prefer operational clarity over dashboard theater.
Layouts should support scanning, comparison, exception handling, filtering, and repeated action. Visual polish is valuable when it makes the work easier to understand.

3. Treat money as a primary object.
Monetary values, percentages, installments, and period totals should be typographically stable, responsive, and legible. Charts must clarify movement and obligation, not merely decorate a card.

4. Keep role context obvious.
Admin, Finance, Manager, EV, and CN views can share a system, but each surface should foreground the decisions that role is responsible for.

5. Preserve traceability and reversibility until lock.
Before a quarterly appraisal is locked, the interface should help users review, recalculate, resolve, and understand exceptions. After lock, it should communicate finality and audit confidence.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Follow responsive, keyboard-usable, contrast-conscious product UI practices. Respect reduced-motion preferences. Controls should have clear focus states, touch targets should remain usable on smaller screens, and data visualizations should not rely on color alone to communicate meaning.
